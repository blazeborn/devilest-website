Featured Project

Netherlords · Solo Developer · In Development since Aug 2025

A dark fantasy action roguelite where your build is a deck of cards. Carve through hordes of fallen souls with soulslike combat, hunt for loot and shape each run card by card. Inspired by Diablo II, Dark Souls and Vampire Survivors, built solo in Unreal Engine 5.

Netherlords is my combat design study: after years of experience, I'm taking a game from zero to release to consolidate that experience and show what I can build end to end.

Unreal Engine 5 Blueprints GAS C++ PCG State Machines MVVM CommonUI

Pillars

Gameplay first

Core mechanics, controls and the combat loop come before story, art and polish. The first playtests evaluated the raw gameplay, with VFX, sound and narrative kept low priority.

Variation

Items, weapons, monsters and elites are data-driven from tables that roll a vast range of variations and modifiers. Runtime PCG and other procedural techniques make exploration feel new every run.

Intense atmosphere

Optimized fluid simulations, dramatic lighting and haunting locations, grounded in Spiritist philosophy and Dantesque literature.

Next: controller-first UI with CommonUI and Enhanced Input, options and multi-character saves, a first-hour onboarding pass, shrine loop, new enemy abilities (ranged, revive, boss moves), balancing and the Steam page with a trailer and a demo.

Combat: Weapons & Moveset Pipeline

Live
Goal

Combat needs variation to keep players engaged, with enough options to stay fun over long sessions.

Problem

A large variety of weapons, each playing differently enough to change how the game is played: 15 weapon bases with their own combos and hybrid attribute scaling, without per-weapon code.

Solution
  • Combo growth. Each weapon base has combos bound to GAS abilities. The moveset starts at 2 hits and grows to 5 with use, rewarding players who stick with a weapon.
  • Attribute scaling. Damage scales with Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence. Some weapons scale best with combined stats, encouraging new combinations of stats and weapons.
  • On-hit effects. Multiple Gameplay Effects or Abilities can be applied on hit from the timeline, such as hit stun, whose duration is calculated from the damage received.
  • Moveset pipeline. From ability to hit reaction.
    1. AbilityBase
    2. ComboBase
    3. Moveset Data Asset
    4. Anim Montage
    5. Timeline notifieshitbox · combo window · FX
    6. On-hit Gameplay Effects
    7. CharacterBase
    8. EnemyBase
    9. Specific enemy
Result

All weapons run on a single implementation, only animations and data change.

A new weapon is a base mesh (skeletal or static), its combo animations, material, quality, rarity, affixes and scaling coefficients for Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence.

Next

Elemental areas where enemies have attack strengths and defense weaknesses, pushing players to swap gear per area.

More animations and a timing polish pass on combos.

Progression: Level, Gear & Cards

Live
Goal

Varied items and progression so each run feels different, and players build their own story from the gear they find.

Problem

One developer has to author and balance enough variation to sustain replayability.

Solution
  • Item pool. 15 weapon bases, 10 rings, 10 armors, 10 amulets and 22 consumables.
  • Quality and rarity. 3 quality tiers × 4 rarities. Gear rolls 1 to 6 affixes from a pool of 55, depending on rarity.
  • Data-driven. Everything is defined in spreadsheet tables (tiers, value ranges, item level, weights, allowed item types) imported as Data Tables, simple to edit and analyze.
  • Level and gear. Level-ups grant attributes and a card pick. Gear is also a major part of progression, granting important stats.
Result

Itemization, progression and balance are very time-consuming, and as a solo developer I need to iterate without rework. With items as data in Data Tables, I can tune several items across several tables at the same time.

Next

Instant level-ups break immersion, while shrine-only level-ups (bonfire-style) can leave players stuck in an open world. Well-placed shrines plus better tutorials and NPC guidance could solve both.

World: Enemies & PCG

Live AI in progress
Goal

Knowing exactly where everything is gets boring, but pure randomness is unreadable. Each area rerolls within its own rules, so players can still anticipate where enemies spawn, what they drop and how they fight, with constant pressure across a large map and no wasted performance.

Problem

Enemies spawn all the time across the whole map, and building and animating many enemy types is costly for one person.

Solution
  • Shared skeleton. The player and many common enemies share the main skeleton (retargeted, some hand-skinned in Blender), so GAS abilities are reused. Custom skeletons are also supported, though they need their own animations and movesets.
  • State machine AI. Enemies run on Logic Driver Pro state machines and seek and hold an ideal distance from the player. State machines make each enemy's decisions easier to visualize and organize. Experienced with Behavior Trees, I chose state machines for this project to test them as an alternative.
  • Spawns and elites. Spawn level and elite chance come from tables, currently scaled to the player's level. Elites can roll extra bonuses such as speed or extra health, sometimes both. They are the real threat, regular enemies alone should not be a strong danger.
  • Safe home. The starting area is fixed, with free loot, planned as the hub for NPC services such as quests and trade. The rest of the map is built to be rerolled every run.
  • Performance. A dormancy component switches off enemies far from the player, and World Partition unloads the ones that are very far away.
  • PCG. Stock nodes handle most spawning, plus a custom node that spawns actor Blueprints so each piece can be modified.
Result

Building a world solo means everything has to be automated and simple. PCG covers much of it, and nothing is hardcoded, every solution works on a new map as-is.

Next

Reconsidering enemy level based on the player (Vampire Survivors) versus the area (Diablo II), more areas, and profiling passes to improve performance.

UI: MVVM & Controller-First Input

Live
Goal

Simple, scalable UI for dozens of enemies and hundreds of cards, working across platforms on both mouse/keyboard and gamepad.

Problem

Keeping a growing roster of enemies and cards in sync with the UI, easy to debug, without rebuilding widgets for every new addition or input device.

Solution
  • Seamless input switching. CommonUI detects the active input device, and button prompts, focus and tooltips swap between gamepad and mouse and keyboard instantly, mid-screen.
  • Controller-first navigation. Menus are CommonUI activatable widgets with focus navigation, and UI actions are bound through Enhanced Input, so the action bar (use, drop, close, navigate) comes from data instead of being hardcoded per screen.
  • Data separated from presentation. The player's status ViewModel is registered globally, so the HUD, character status and inventory all bind to the same instance. Enemies reuse that ViewModel class, created manually per enemy for their health bars.
  • Deck builder. Its own ViewModel manages decks and selection, and a virtualized TileView keeps 200+ cards smooth.
Result

Gameplay classes don't need to reference widgets directly, and the ViewModel works as a reliable, organized data source. New enemies and cards use the existing UI without extra code, and UI bugs are traced to a single place. CommonUI handles input routing, focus and platform prompts across devices.

Next

Localization pass on UI texts, with text overflow checks.

Development Pipeline

Live
Goal

A large-scale project that stays performant and maintainable as content grows.

Problem

A large procedurally generated world kept loaded, dozens of enemies on screen at once, hundreds of generated items and modern UE5 rendering, all maintained by a single developer.

Solution
  • Load dependencies. Heavy Blueprints are referenced through native C++ parent classes and soft references, so each asset loads only what it uses. The player Blueprint dropped from 1.1 GiB to 211 MiB of load dependencies.
  • Performance & profiling. Unreal Insights traces to find bottlenecks, then fix them at the source: expensive actor ticks, Niagara culling by distance, texture memory reduction.
  • Modular architecture.
    • Subsystems for global services (item generation, spawn director, loading screen, save).
    • Characters built from components (inventory, card management, music).
    • Data-driven content: enemies, items, affixes and movesets tuned in tables, no code needed.
  • Development environment.
    • Self-hosted Perforce on a dedicated machine, accessible from anywhere through a secure zero-trust tunnel.
    • Engine-level changes such as version upgrades go through a separate branch as a safeguard, stabilized there and then merged into the main line.
  • AI-assisted development. Built with an LLM (Claude) as a pair-programming partner for debugging, code review and research. Every change is carefully designed and reviewed by me before submit.
  • Expanded native Unreal systems. C++ foundations extended in Blueprints on top of GAS (abilities, effects, custom damage execution), MVVM and CommonUI, Enhanced Input and PCG.
Result

Stable playtest builds after more than a year of solo development, with ~19,000 lines of C++ across 170+ submitted Perforce changelists. Dozens of enemies, hundreds of card modifiers and thousands of possible items, and more to come with minimal rework, thanks to the modular, data-driven setup.

Debugging

Bug Hunts

  • ReproducePlaytest until it is clear how the issue happens.
  • InvestigatePick the right tool: breakpoints (C++ or Blueprint), crash logs, Unreal Insights, Widget Reflector, Reference Viewer, built-in or custom debuggers, or Print String.
  • FixWeigh each possible fix against its impact on other systems before it goes in.

Examples

Enemies ignored the player

IssueAfter refactoring the player and enemies into separate parent classes (both still derived from the same C++ base), enemies stopped reacting to the player.

InvestigationThe Gameplay Debugger's perception view showed enemies seeing the player, but nothing was triggered.

Root causeThe player character lost its AIPerceptionStimuliSource in the refactor, so it was never registered as a stimulus.

FixAdded the stimuli source back to the player character.

Regen effect silently inhibited

IssueHealth, Stamina and Mana regen weren't applying, and with one effect per resource they were hard to track.

InvestigationThe GAS Debugger showed the effect applied to the character but flagged as !!!Inhibited.

Root causePeriod was set to 0 instead of left unset, and periodic ticking interfered with the MMC scaling regen from attributes.

FixSplit regen into two effects: one for the tick, one for attribute scaling.

Damage not matching the formula

IssueDamage dealt didn't match what the formula should produce. The execution calculates:

Raw = WeaponBase + (STR×STRScale + DEX×DEXScale + INT×INTScale) × WeaponBase / 100
Physical = Raw × (1 − Defense / (Defense + 100))

plus elemental damage, each reduced by its own resistance.

InvestigationTraced back every input to the damage effect: the captured attributes (weapon base, Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, weapon scaling, target defense), the SetByCaller values and which effect sets each one. Used the GAS Debugger, breakpoints, and the execution's log line that prints every term of the formula.

Root causeBonus modifiers feeding the calculation were applied as Add (Base), so each recalculation of the base value overwrote them.

FixSwitched those modifiers to Add (Final).

Ranged enemies attacking up close

IssueRanged enemies closed in and attacked from melee range, throwing away their advantage.

InvestigationReproduced it and watched the Logic Driver Pro state machine at runtime. Enemies stayed locked at the closest distance instead of seeking one that suited their moveset.

FixPreferred combat distance is now derived at runtime from the enemy's granted abilities. Each ability declares its own valid range band in the data table, and the AI positions itself where its current moveset is actually usable. Enemies are table-driven, so behaviour changes per creature without touching a single Blueprint.

Startup crash in packaged builds (v0.8)

IssueIn some cases the game crashed when loading the gameplay map, only in packaged builds.

InvestigationPackaged a Development build to get crash logs, which pointed to specific skeletal meshes.

Root causeSkeletal meshes using the GPU Skin Cache on the non-Nanite Virtual Shadow Map path.

Fixes evaluatedDisabling shadows and then Skin Cache on those meshes stopped the crash, but at a visual and performance cost.

FixEnabled Nanite on those meshes, which kept the visuals and can improve performance.
